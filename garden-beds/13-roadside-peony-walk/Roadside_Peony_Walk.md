# 🌸 Greenshadow Roadside Peony Walk

> A long, narrow roadside ribbon along Greenshadow Ct — color-drift peonies as the spring centerpiece, layered with companions and fall bulbs for ~10 months of interest, plus a little dog rest-stop (bench + hydrant + bag dispenser) as a gift to the neighborhood.

> **Status (May 2026):** Conceptual — design complete, planting targeted for **Fall 2026 (late Sept–Oct)**. Land ownership confirmed (not right-of-way). Exact strip dimensions TBD — quantities below are per-30-ft and get multiplied once measured.

---

## Location & Conditions

- **Where:** The long, narrow strip running parallel to Greenshadow Ct, on the **high (road-side) shoulder** above the drainage swale. West edge of the property.
- **Shape:** Linear ribbon — a "walk," not a bed. Plant a single staggered row (or tight double row if width allows).
- **Exposure:** **Full sun** ✅
- **Soil:** NC Piedmont clay, slightly acidic. Plant **up on the high side, out of the swale low point** — peonies rot in wet feet. Berm/amend with compost + grit for drainage where needed.
- **Zone:** 7b
- **The swale:** Curving drainage line along the strip's length — carries road runoff. Keep peonies and perennials up out of it; reserve the damper low edge for moisture-tolerant bulbs (camassia).
- **The tree (bottom/south end):** Throws shade — stop the peony drifts at its dripline or keep only the sunniest gaps planted there.
- **Dimensions:** **TBD** — measure plantable length (tree → top run) and width (one row vs. two).

## The Concept

A roadside ribbon that reads "intentional and lovely" from a passing car, does real pollinator work up close, and peaks with a **color-drift peony show** each May. Companions and fall bulbs carry the other seasons so it's never bare. A mid-strip **rest node** (bench + dog hydrant + poop-bag dispenser) turns it into a neighborhood dog-walking stop.

Layout reads: **peony drifts → rest node → more peony drifts.**

---

## Color Strategy: Drifts (not a rainbow mix)

Plant peonies in **blocks of 3–5 of one color**, then shift down the length — e.g. **white → blush → pink → white** — repeating. Gives variety *and* cohesion; far more deliberate than a spotty single-of-each mix when viewed from the road.

## Fall-Planted Bulbs (tuck between everything in October)

All **deer/vole-resistant and naturalizing** — essential for an unprotected roadside.

| Bulb | Latin | Bloom | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Daffodils** | *Narcissus* | Early spring | Bloom *before* peonies wake; totally critter-proof; naturalize over years. |
| **Muscari (grape hyacinth)** | *Muscari* | Early spring | Blue carpet between drifts. |
| **Crocus / Snowdrops** | *Crocus* / *Galanthus* | Late winter | First color of the year. |
| **Alliums** | *Allium* | Late spring | Purple globes bloom *with* late peonies; dried seedheads persist for weeks. |
| **Camassia** | *Camassia* | Late spring | Tolerates the **damper swale-edge soil** — plant on the low side. |

> ⚠️ **Skip tulips** — deer candy and short-lived (effectively annuals) in 7b heat.

## The Dog Rest-Stop (mid-strip node)

A clustered "destination" that interrupts the peony drifts and gives back to dog-walkers.

- **Placement:** A **level, sunny stretch on the high side**, away from the shade tree and out of the swale. Set back a little from traffic — avoid any blind-curve spot. (Eyeball in person.)
- **Bench:** On a **gravel or flagstone pad** (no muddy clay, no mowing under it). Weatherproof material — powder-coated metal or recycled poly. Face toward the peonies or the street, whichever view's better.
- **Dog fire hydrant:** At the **curb edge, a few feet from the bench.**
  - ⚠️ **Dog urine kills plants** — surround the hydrant with **gravel or tough turf**, NOT peonies/perennials. Keep the prized drifts well back.
- **Poop-bag dispenser:** Mounted on a **post by the bench** (or the hydrant post). **Plan to refill it** — an empty dispenser looks neglected.
- **Backing:** A flowering shrub or grass clump behind the bench makes the node feel tucked-in and intentional.
