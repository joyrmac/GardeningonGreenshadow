## ⚠️ Important: Your Two Beds Want DIFFERENT Care

Because of the variety mix, the two beds need different runner/flower management:

### Bed 2 (all Seascape — everbearing/day-neutral)
- **REMOVE runners** as they appear (snip at base) — these fruit on the mother crown; runners just drain energy
- **Pinch flowers until ~July 1**, then let them fruit for a fall harvest THIS year
- "Hill system" — bigger berries, more per plant

### Bed 1 (mixed: 15 Albion day-neutral + 10 Chandler June-bearing)
This bed is split-personality. Two approaches:

**Option A (simplest — treat the whole bed as day-neutral):**
- Remove runners from BOTH Albion and Chandler
- Pinch flowers till July, then let everything fruit
- You sacrifice Chandler's matted-row potential but keep the bed tidy

**Option B (treat each variety separately):**
- **Albion (day-neutral):** remove runners, pinch till July
- **Chandler (June-bearing):** let runners root and fill in (matted row), pinch ALL flowers year 1, big harvest year 2
- More work but maximizes each variety

**Recommendation:** Option A for simplicity year 1. You can get fancier in year 2 once you see how each behaves.

## First-Year Care Summary (2026)

- ✅ **Mulch with STRAW** (not hay — hay has weed seeds). Keeps berries off wet soil, insulates roots, prevents fungal rot. Apply now if not done.
- ✅ **Pinch early flowers** — day-neutrals until July 1; Chandler all year-1 flowers (if doing matted row)
- ✅ **Runner management** per the bed split above
- 💧 **Water deeply, 1–1.5"/week** — strawberries are shallow-rooted, need consistent moisture
- 🚫 **No heavy fertilizer year 1** — light feeding only; too much nitrogen = leaves not fruit

## 🌿 Companion Planting

### ⭐ Top companion: BORAGE (you have 3 seed packs! #62)

Borage is THE classic strawberry companion:
- Improves strawberry **flavor and yield** (long-documented)
- Attracts pollinators → better fruit set
- Deters pests
- Adds trace minerals to the soil
- Edible blue star-flowers

**Action:** Tuck a few borage plants among / around the edges of both strawberry beds.

### Other good companions (mostly from inventory)

| Plant | Benefit | Have it? |
|---|---|---|
| **Marigolds** | Deter root-knot nematodes (real strawberry pest) | ✅ Multiple varieties |
| **Thyme** | Repels worms, low groundcover, pest deterrent | ✅ Lemon Thyme in Spiral (divide) |
| **Calendula** | Pollinator draw + pest deterrent | ✅ Seeds #160, #161 |
| **Chives / onions / garlic** | Prevent fungal disease, deter aphids | Check inventory |
| **Sage** | General pest deterrent | ✅ In Spiral |
| **Spinach / lettuce** | Low-growing, non-competitive, edible interplant | — |
| **Bush beans** | Fix nitrogen (strawberries are heavy feeders) | — |

### ❌ Do NOT plant near strawberries

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| **Brassicas** (cabbage, broccoli, kale, Brussels sprouts) | Compete heavily, stunt strawberry growth |
| **Nightshades** (tomatoes, peppers, potatoes, eggplant) | Share Verticillium wilt — disease transmission risk |
| **Fennel** | Allelopathic — inhibits neighbors |
| **Mint** | Aggressively overruns the bed (unless in a buried pot) |

**⚠️ Keep the strawberry patch well away from the Wildwood tomato beds** — Verticillium wilt risk.

## Ongoing Care (Year 2+)

- **After fruiting (June–July):** "Renovate" June-bearing beds — mow tops to 1", thin plants to ~6" spacing, top-dress with compost
- **Day-neutrals:** keep removing runners; they fruit 2–3 years then decline — replace as needed
- **Fall:** mulch heavily with straw for winter protection (Zone 7b strawberries appreciate it)
- **Spring:** pull back winter mulch as growth resumes, side-dress with compost
- **Every 3–4 years:** strawberry beds decline; start a new bed from runners/new plants and rotate
