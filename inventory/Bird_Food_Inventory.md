## 🐿️ Squirrel Feeding Station (Under the Tree)

> A dedicated "peace offering" station to draw squirrels away from the bird feeders by the screen room.

### Setup

- **Location:** Under the tree (the old bird feeder spot)
- **Distance from bird feeders:** Aim for 20–30 ft minimum so it pulls squirrels away, not launches them toward the birds
- **Feeder style options:**
  - Flat platform / picnic-table style feeder mounted on tree trunk or low post
  - Corn cob spike holder (screw-mount or hanging)
  - Wooden box feeder with a lift-up lid (squirrels learn to open it — cute show)
- **Water:** Small shallow dish nearby (refresh every couple days)

### What to Feed Them

| Food | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Dried field corn on the cob** | Cheap in bulk at Tractor Supply, lasts forever, mounts on spikes |
| **Whole peanuts in shell** | Unsalted, raw or roasted (no salt!) — squirrels love cracking them |
| **Black oil sunflower seeds** | Calorie-dense, beloved by squirrels |
| **Acorns / hickory nuts** | If you can collect them in fall, save for winter feeding |
| **Pumpkin & squash seeds** | Save from kitchen scraps, dry them out |
| **Apple or pear slices** | Occasional treat, remove before they rot |
| **Carrots, sweet potato chunks** | Good for variety, especially in winter |

### What to **NEVER** Feed

- ❌ Salted or roasted-salted nuts
- ❌ Bread, crackers, cereal (junk food — no nutrition)
- ❌ Candy, chocolate, sugary anything
- ❌ Avocado (toxic to squirrels)
- ❌ Onion, garlic, raw potato
- ❌ Almonds in large quantities (contain trace cyanide compounds)
- ❌ Cashews (low nutritional value, can cause calcium deficiency)
- ❌ Dairy

### Tips

- **Rotate the menu** — variety keeps them interested and well-nourished
- **Don't overfeed** — a handful daily is plenty; you want to supplement, not replace their foraging
- **Watch for chipmunks** — they'll show up too, which is a bonus
- **Winter is when they need it most** — keep it stocked Dec–Feb when natural food is scarce
- **Summer:** lighter portions, more fresh produce, always fresh water
- **Name the regulars** — half the fun is recognizing the chunky one, the bossy one, the polite one 🐿️
- **Keep it clean** — sweep out shells and old food weekly to avoid mold and rodents
- **Squirrel-cam idea:** if you want to monitor activity, a cheap motion-trail camera pointed at the station is a fun project

## 🌶️ Spicy Seed Recipe (Squirrel Deterrent)

### Why it works
- Birds lack the **TRPV1 receptor** that mammals have, so they literally cannot taste capsaicin
- Squirrels, raccoons, deer, and chipmunks taste it and learn to avoid the feeder
- Totally safe for birds when applied correctly

### Method 1: Oil + Cayenne (Joy's preferred — better stickiness ⭐)

Best for windy spots and tube feeders where dry powder blows off and can irritate bird eyes.

1. Pour seed into a 5-gallon bucket with a lid
2. Add a **light drizzle of oil** — about **1 tbsp per 5 lb of seed**, no more
   - **Good oils:** mineral oil, food-grade safflower oil, or store-bought capsaicin oil products (e.g. Cole's Flaming Squirrel Seed Sauce)
   - **Avoid:** olive oil and vegetable oils (go rancid fast in summer heat)
3. Snap the lid on and shake hard for 30 seconds to coat evenly
4. Sprinkle in cayenne — **2–3 tbsp per 5 lb of seed** — shake again
5. **Spread the seed in a single layer on a baking sheet or tarp and let it dry for several hours to overnight** — this is the critical step. The seed should feel tacky but not dripping
6. Store the dried spicy seed in a sealed bucket, separate from regular seed
7. Refill feeders as normal

### Method 2: Dry Cayenne Only (Simpler, but dusty)

Works fine for hopper-style and covered feeders where powder doesn't blow around as much.

1. Pour seed into a bucket with a lid
2. Sprinkle in cayenne — **2–3 tbsp per 5 lb** to start
3. Shake to mix and refill feeders

### Method 3: Buy Pre-Made

- **Cole's Flaming Squirrel Seed Sauce** — capsaicin oil concentrate, just drizzle on any seed
- **Hot-pepper suet cakes** — already infused, no mixing needed
- **Hot-pepper coated seed** — sold by the bag at some Wild Birds Unlimited locations

### Pro tips
- **Wear gloves AND eye protection** when mixing — capsaicin on your hands or eyes is miserable
- **Mix outside on a calm day**, ideally wearing a mask
- **Light coating wins** — heavy oil can transfer to bird feathers in rainy weather and damage waterproofing. Less is more.
- **Skip during heavy rain / cold snaps** — wet capsaicin oil + perches + wet bird = potential feather issue. Use dry method or pre-made cakes in winter.
- **Refresh per refill** — coating wears off over time
- **Label the bucket clearly** so no one mistakes it for regular seed

## 🎙️ Bird Audio Gear (for Merlin Bird ID)

> External mic setup to boost the Merlin Bird ID app on iPhone (USB-C). Goal: capture the **full bird chorus** with omnidirectional/stereo pickup, not target one bird.

### The Kit

| Item | Price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Zoom H1essential** ⭐ | ~$100 | Handheld recorder w/ stereo X/Y mics, USB-C, 32-bit float (no clipping) |
| **Furry windjammer** (Movo WST20) | ~$20 | Critical for outdoor — omni mics catch wind from every direction |
| **USB-C to USB-C cable** (short, 1 ft) | ~$8 | Connects recorder to iPhone as external mic |
| **64GB microSD card** | ~$10 | For standalone recording (leave it on the porch for hours) |
| **Total** | **~$138** | |

### Why the Zoom H1essential

- Plugs into the iPhone via USB-C — Merlin uses it as the mic input automatically
- **Stereo X/Y mics** capture spatial soundscape (you can hear *where* each bird is)
- **32-bit float** means even a screaming blue jay right next to it won't clip the audio
- Doubles as a **standalone recorder** — set it on the screen room rail, hit record, review later
- Palm-sized, runs on AAs or USB power

### Tips for Best Merlin Results

- **Quiet body** — bird audio picks up clothing rustle, breathing, footsteps
- **Early morning, low wind** — dawn chorus is peak ID time
- **Hold steady or set down** — hand movement creates noise
- **Closer is better** than fancier gear — 10 ft closer beats a $200 mic upgrade
- **Standalone mode for screen room** — leave it recording during your morning coffee, scrub through later in Merlin's Sound ID

### Skip These

- ❌ Shotgun mics (directional — you want wide pickup)
- ❌ Lavalier / clip-on (too close-mic, catches clothing)
- ❌ Desktop podcast mics like Rode NT-USB Mini (not built for outdoors)

### Upgrade Path (Later)

If you fall deep into bird audio:
- **Tula Mic Linear** (~$269) — premium omni USB-C, retro design that fits the 90s aesthetic
- **Wildtronics Mini Parabolic** (~$200) — for targeting specific distant birds
- **Sennheiser Ambeo Smart Headset** — 3D binaural recording (overkill but wow)
